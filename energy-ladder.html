How these numbers were built — assumptions & sources

This is a like-for-like model, not a spec sheet. Real consumption swings with speed, terrain, weather, rider weight and driving style, so treat every figure as a well-supported typical value for temperate, mixed conditions — good for comparing modes against each other, not for predicting your exact next trip.

The five metrics

Onboard energy (kWh/100 km) — energy drawn from the tank or battery. For fuels, litres × calorific value. For electric and human power, measured consumption at the wheel/battery. Well-to-wheel energy — onboard energy plus the energy lost getting fuel or electricity there: ×1.20 for petrol/diesel (extraction, refining, distribution), ×1.155 for electricity (grid delivery + charging losses), and ×5 for human power (the fossil energy embodied in modern food). Drivetrain efficiency — share of stored onboard energy that reaches the wheels. Running cost and CO₂ are computed from onboard energy using the prices and carbon factors below.

Prices are indicative Slovenian figures for 2026; the grid-carbon value is a European-average — on a coal-heavy grid an EV's CO₂ roughly doubles, on a renewable or nuclear grid it falls close to zero. Human-power rows show metabolic energy above rest; their cost and CO₂ are left blank because "buying food to pedal" isn't how anyone budgets a trip, though the food-system footprint is real (hence the ×5 well-to-wheel factor). Shared-transport rows (bus, train) are per passenger at typical occupancy and will look very different on a full or near-empty vehicle.
